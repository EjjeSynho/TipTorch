# ETC-style NFM calibrator

The goal is to create a training and validation routine for calibrated PSF prediction in the context of an exposure time calculator (ETC). In this regime, the model has access only to inputs such as the median $\tau_0$, seeing, $C_n^2$ profile, etc. In other words, the ETC feature space is much smaller than the input space of the current NFM calibrator, which is designed to run after observations in the PSF reconstruction (PSF-R) regime.

## Plan

- Analyze the most meaningful input features for the NFM calibrator;
- Determine the input spaces for the ETC and PSF-R cases;
- Identify whether the most important PSF-R features can be predicted from the ETC features;
- Decide what to use in place of the less meaningful missing features;
- Design an ETC-friendly calibrator;
- Train it;
- Validate it;

You are free to alter this plan as you want if necessary. See it as a recommendation.


## Methods

- Determine which strategy is best:
    * Train a brand-new calibrator that replaces the current calibrator, uses ETC inputs instead of PSF-R inputs, and connects directly to the TipTorch PSF model;
    * Train another small model that precedes the current calibrator and feeds it predictions for the missing parameters based on the ETC inputs.

    I'd personally favor the second option, even though it's more cumbersome, but PSF prediction accuracy must be the priority. If the first option is chosen, you may ignore the irrelevant parts of the plan.

- Since we're trying to match the distributions, you may approach the problem probabilistically.


## Outputs

The output must be a joint training and validation routine for prediction in the ETC regime. The distribution of the parameters of ETC PSFs generated by the calibrated TipTorch should match the on-sky distribution as closely as possible in terms of ETC-relevant PSF parameter distributions. The new predictions must also be compared with those from Enrico's routines.

## Purpose / Big Picture

After this change, calibrated TipTorch can generate MUSE NFM PSFs from ETC inputs only (seeing, tau0, airmass, NGS magnitude and
the turbulence category), such that the distribution of the ETC-relevant PSF parameters (FWHM, Strehl, encircled energy) over many
observations matches the on-sky distribution. Observable through:

- `python machine_learning/train_ETC_calibrator.py` builds the ETC bundle `data/weights/ETC_calibrator/ETC_calibrator_bundle.pth`;
- `python machine_learning/validate_ETC_calibrator.py` replays the validation split in the ETC regime for every predictor, measures
  the PSFs with the ETC metrics, compares them with the on-sky PSFs and with vanilla TIPTOP run on the same conditions, and writes
  `data/Enrico_ETC/ETC_calibrator/comparison_summary.md` and the figures in `data/Enrico_ETC/plots/ETC_calibrator/`;
- `python development/run_tiptorch_random_carlo.py --predictor donor-params` regenerates the Monte Carlo of the ETC comparison with
  the chosen predictor, so that `ETC_compute_PSF_params.py --source tiptorch --folder ...` and `ETC_compare_distributions.py
  --tiptorch-dir ...` compare it with Enrico's TIPTOP Monte Carlo.

## Feature analysis (validation split, 107 observations; `data/Enrico_ETC/ETC_calibrator/features_report.md`)

Input spaces. ETC inputs (6): header seeing, header tau0, telescope altitude (airmass), NGS magnitude, IRLOS frequency and gain
(the regime follows from the magnitude). Site / profile (15): MASS/DIMM and Cn2-profile features, ASM wind, LGS flux; the ETC
replaces them by the TC median profile (`tc_profile_telemetry`). AO diagnostics (5): LGS_STREHL, LGS_TURVAR_RES, LGS_FWHM_GAIN,
LGS_SLOPERMS, theta0. Nuisance (5): exposure time, azimuth, parallactic and derotator angle, temperature.

Importance for the PSF-R calibrator (gradient importance, mean |d out / d in| over the validation telemetry): the AO diagnostics
carry 21-34 % of the importance of every predicted parameter with only 5 of 31 features (LGS_STREHL, LGS_TURVAR_RES and LGS_TUR_ALT
are the three most important single features, telescope altitude the fourth); the ETC inputs 17-22 %; the 15 site / profile
features 35-45 %; the nuisances 10-16 %.

Predictability from the ETC inputs (grouped 5-fold CV R2, random forest): LGS_STREHL 0.59, LGS_SLOPERMS 0.48, theta0 0.41,
LGS_TURVAR_RES 0.23, LGS_FWHM_GAIN 0.07; MASS tau0 / turbulence velocity / IA_FWHMLINOBS 0.60-0.65, free-atmosphere seeing 0.45,
the Cn2 fractions and altitudes, wind direction and LGS flux < 0.1; exposure time 0.79, temperature 0.22, the pointing angles
< 0.13. The most important missing features are therefore only partly predictable: their conditional distribution given the ETC
inputs has a large residual spread, which a deterministic replacement (imputer, conditional mean) removes.

## Decision Log

- Decision: the ETC predictor is evaluated as a *distribution matcher*, not a per-sample predictor. The validation replays the
  real validation observations with only their ETC inputs, generates PSFs, measures them with the ETC metrics
  (`development/ETC_psf_metrics.py`: Enrico's FWHM, PSFAO Strehl, TIPTOP-style peak Strehl and EE50 on the 100 px field) and
  compares the per-TC / per-wavelength distributions with those of the on-sky PSFs of the same observations (dataset cubes,
  identical code) by Wasserstein-1 distance normalized by the on-sky IQR; the paired MAE / Spearman rho are reported alongside.
  Rationale: the plan's output criterion is the match of the ETC-relevant parameter distributions; the on-sky scatter at fixed ETC
  conditions is dominated by features the ETC does not know, so a per-sample error is not the right objective.
  Date/Author: 2026-10-12, Claude.
- Decision: both strategies of the plan are implemented behind one interface and compared on the same validation (strategy B:
  `CalibratorPredictor` = PSF-R calibrator fed with telemetry completed by the imputer / a conditional Gaussian / a nearest-neighbour
  donor observation; strategy A: `DonorParamsPredictor` = fitted PSF-model parameters of a nearest-neighbour donor with r0 and wind
  rescaled to the requested seeing / tau0, and `DirectNetPredictor` = a 6-input network trained end-to-end with the PSF model).
  Rationale: the plan asks to determine the best strategy with PSF accuracy as the priority; the shared renderer and metrics make
  the comparison fair and the choice reversible.
  Date/Author: 2026-10-12, Claude.
- Decision: donors are drawn among the k = 10 nearest training observations in the standardized space of the six ETC inputs
  (hot-deck sampling); the pool is the training split only, so the validation stays out of sample.
  Rationale: a non-parametric conditional draw preserves the joint distribution of the unknown features and of the fitted
  parameters, including their non-Gaussian tails (poor AO nights), which the imputer and the Gaussian model shrink.
  Date/Author: 2026-10-12, Claude.
- Decision: the ETC configs use the dataset median Cn2 layer heights [0, 8.6, 18.3] km (`heights_policy='median'`) for the
  calibrator-based and the direct-network predictors, and the donor's own heights for the donor-parameter predictors; the TIPTOP
  "white profile" heights [0, 2, 20] km are kept only as the `impute-white` baseline (the previous Monte Carlo recipe).
  Rationale: the calibrator was trained with the real profile heights, its predicted weights belong to them.
  Date/Author: 2026-10-12, Claude.
- Decision: the phase bump (first LO coefficient, not predicted) is set to the training median (35 nm) in all ETC predictors
  instead of the 0 that `NFMCalibrator` uses at inference.
  Rationale: training re-attaches the fitted bump; the effect on the ETC metrics is small (about 1-2 mas in FWHM) but the median
  is the consistent choice.
  Date/Author: 2026-10-12, Claude.
- Decision: all ETC work ran on the idle GPU 1 (`CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=1`) with its own weights folder `data/weights/ETC_calibrator/`,
  because the user's PSF-R calibrator training was running on GPU 0 and writing `data/weights/NFM_calibrator/` at the same time.
  Date/Author: 2026-10-12, Claude.

## Surprises & Discoveries

- Observation: the PSF-R calibrator itself, fed with the real telemetry and the real configs, already compresses the on-sky FWHM
  distribution: at 700 nm the predicted spread is 0.55 of the on-sky spread with a bias of -9 mas (MAE 11 mas, Spearman rho 0.63),
  while the per-sample *fitted* parameters reproduce the on-sky metrics almost exactly (FWHM 80.1 vs 81.0 mas, peak Strehl 5.9 vs
  5.4 % on the first validation samples). Evidence: `feature_ablation.csv`, row "real telemetry"; the fitted-parameter check in
  the session log. Consequence: the model can represent the on-sky PSFs, the deficit of strategy B is inherited from the
  calibrator's regression to the mean, and any predictor that goes through the calibrator keeps a too-narrow FWHM distribution.
- Observation: replacing feature groups by the trained imputer changes the *distributions* of the calibrator output very little
  (FWHM W1 8.9-9.5 mas for every ablation against 9.3 mas with the real telemetry); only the per-sample correlation degrades
  (rho 0.63 -> 0.33 for FWHM when all non-ETC features are imputed). Consequence: for a distribution-matching ETC the information
  lost by the ETC reduction is a second-order effect compared with the calibrator's own compression.
- Observation: on-sky metrics measured on the dataset cubes agree with Enrico's table for the same observations only to rho 0.65
  (FWHM at 700 nm, N = 82) with a few outliers (e.g. 120 vs 54 mas), and Enrico's own distribution scores W1 / IQR 0.4-0.7 against
  the dataset-cube measurement. Consequence: the measurement itself sets a noise floor of roughly half an IQR for the comparison;
  the dataset cubes are the reference because they are what the calibrator was trained to reproduce.
- Observation: `shap` cannot be imported in the `TipTorch` env (pyarrow / GLIBCXX); the importance analysis uses autograd gradient
  magnitudes instead, and the ablation of feature groups measured in PSF metrics.
- Observation: `PSFModelNFM` scales r0 and the layer heights by the airmass internally (`r0_ = r0 * airmass^(-3/5)`), so the
  fitted zenith r0 of a donor observation can be reused at another airmass; `sensor_LO NumberPhotons` is not used by the NFM
  model (no LO noise term), so the NGS-magnitude dependence of the jitter can only come from the predictors (which condition on it).
- Observation: the phase bump of `NFMCalibrator` at inference is 0, while training uses the per-sample fitted value (median 35 nm);
  the effect on the ETC metrics is about 1-2 mas of FWHM.

## Outcomes & Retrospective

Validation on the 107 held-out observations (`data/Enrico_ETC/ETC_calibrator/comparison_summary.md`; score = Wasserstein-1
distance to the on-sky distribution / on-sky IQR, mean over the 4 parameters, 5 wavelengths and the TCs with >= 8 PSFs):

    PSF-R calibrator on the real telemetry (ceiling)   0.40
    donor-params (fitted parameters of a kNN donor)    0.46     direct-net 0.48     donor-profile 0.48
    donor (telemetry hot-deck -> calibrator)           0.56     gaussian 0.57       donor-params-nearest 0.57
    gaussian-mean 0.60   donor-mean 0.62   Enrico's measurement of the same PSFs 0.60
    vanilla TIPTOP (Enrico's tuned config)             0.75
    impute (previous recipe, median heights)           0.88     impute-white (previous recipe) 0.95

All TCs, mean over wavelengths (on-sky median / predictor median, FWHM in mas, Strehl in %): FWHM 64.7 / 61.6 for donor-params,
59.1 direct-net, 64.6 TIPTOP, 54.3 impute, 57.6 PSF-R; peak Strehl 9.7 / 11.2 donor-params, 10.9 direct-net, 12.9 TIPTOP, 14.8
impute. donor-params is the only ETC predictor whose FWHM spread and tail match the on-sky ones (KS 0.18 against 0.62 for impute),
at the price of a weak per-sample correlation (rho 0.21 for FWHM, 0.5-0.6 for the Strehls and EE50), as expected for a draw from
a conditional distribution. The deterministic variants (`*-mean`, `-nearest`, direct-net) correlate better per sample but keep
too-narrow distributions; direct-net is the best deterministic ETC predictor and the best for EE50.

Decision on the strategy: **strategy A in its non-parametric form (`donor-params`)** is adopted as the ETC predictor, with
`direct-net` as the deterministic alternative; strategy B (`donor`, `gaussian`) is implemented and kept but is bounded by the
PSF-R calibrator's own compression of the distributions (0.40 ceiling with the real telemetry) and lands at 0.56-0.62. The
previous imputation recipe is the worst option and the TIPTOP "white profile" heights make it slightly worse again.

Monte Carlo comparison with Enrico's TIPTOP (`data/Enrico_ETC/plots/ETC_comparison_summary_donor-params.md` and
`_replay_donor-params`, `_validation_donor-params`, `_direct-net`): see the figures `ETC_hist_*_donor-params.png`,
`ETC_summary_medians_donor-params.png`, `ETC_distance_heatmap_donor-params.png`. Cells (5 TC x 5 wavelengths) in which each
simulator is closer to the on-sky distribution (TIPTOP / calibrated TipTorch), to be compared with the 2026-10-11 result of the
previous recipe (random sampling: FWHM 22/3, PSFAO Strehl 25/0, peak Strehl 15/10, EE50 10/15):

    donor-params, random sampling, all on-sky PSFs:   FWHM 10/15, Strehl 10/15, peak Strehl 0/25, EE50 0/25
    donor-params, random sampling, validation only:   FWHM 13/12, Strehl 12/13, peak Strehl 1/24, EE50 4/21
    donor-params, on-sky-conditions replay:           FWHM 16/9,  Strehl 12/13, peak Strehl 4/21, EE50 1/24
    direct-net,   random sampling, all on-sky PSFs:   FWHM 10/15, Strehl 10/15, peak Strehl 1/24, EE50 2/23

Medians at 700 nm over all TCs (random sampling): FWHM on-sky 60.0 mas, TIPTOP 66.5, TipTorch donor-params 61.5 (previous recipe
48.5); peak Strehl 8.0 / 7.5 / 6.9 % (previous 14.6); EE50 0.27 / 0.24 / 0.21 (previous 0.33). The calibrated Monte Carlo now
reproduces the on-sky distributions better than Enrico's tuned TIPTOP for the Strehl and encircled-energy parameters at every
wavelength and matches it for the FWHM (TIPTOP's wavelength-dependent jitter tuning was fitted on these same on-sky FWHMs).

Limitations: (1) the donor pool cannot extrapolate beyond the observed conditions (airmass mostly < 1.3, NGS 9-15 mag; the
random Monte Carlo samples airmass 1-2 and NGS 9-16 uniformly, where the nearest donors are reused); the physics of TipTorch
still scales r0 and the layer heights with the sampled airmass, but the jitter and the AO residual come from the donor. (2) The
measurement noise floor (Enrico's values vs the dataset cubes) is about half an IQR, comparable to the best scores. (3) Three
draws per stochastic predictor; the scores of the stochastic predictors fluctuate by a few hundredths between seeds.
(4) The direct network was trained once (no hyper-parameter search); its validation PSF loss is clearly above the PSF-R one.
